Beyond Economics

Two things keep me away from the estimation output, and both of them reward patience more than cleverness.

Guitar

I play guitar. It is the one practice I have kept up across every city I have lived in, and the only one where progress is entirely uncorrelated with how much I already know about it. Learning a piece properly is slow, unglamorous work, the same bar again at half speed, which turns out to be surprisingly good preparation for empirical research.

Surfing

I surf. Living in Lima means the Pacific is a short walk downhill, and the city's breaks are forgiving enough to be a habit rather than an expedition. Surfing is a useful corrective for anyone who spends their working life trying to identify causal effects: the ocean is a system you can read but never control, and the only sensible strategy is to wait, watch, and commit fully when the moment comes.

Elsewhere

Beyond that: applied mathematics, which I am currently studying formally, and which I have come to enjoy for its own sake as much as for what it does for my research; and languages, which I collect slowly. Spanish natively, English and Portuguese with some confidence, French with rather less.
