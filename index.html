About

I am a labor economist. My work asks what happens to workers when the demand for what they do changes faster than they can move, and what social protection systems owe them when it does.

Most of my research concerns Latin American labor markets, where roughly half of all workers are informal, so the usual margins of adjustment operate on only part of the labor force. My dissertation, supervised by Javier Olivera Angulo, estimates the causal effect of generative artificial intelligence on employment, wages and informality across eight Latin American countries. See research.

I am an economics consultant in the Social Protection and Labor Markets Division of the Inter-American Development Bank, and I lecture at the Graduate School of Universidad Peruana de Ciencias Aplicadas. Earlier I worked at the IDB in Washington, DC, and for seven years at the Central Reserve Bank of Peru.

Research interests

  • Labor markets in developing economies: informality, wage structures, occupational mobility
  • The future of work and technological change
  • Artificial intelligence and labor demand
  • Social protection as a response to labor market disruption: pensions, cash transfers, coverage
  • Applied microeconometrics, machine learning and NLP for labor market measurement
